Biometric Verification Against Identity Fraud: A Guide for Lenders

In many markets a national ID card proves little. Cards are forged, bought or borrowed, a person can hold several, and many borrowers have none at all. For a lender this turns into one specific loss: the same person taking loans under different names and never repaying any of them. Biometric verification is the cheapest reliable fix, if it is set up for that problem rather than copied from a bank in a country with good ID.

Three checks, three different problems

Is this the account holder? Verification, 1:1. At login or before a payout, compare a selfie with the face enrolled at onboarding. This stops account takeover and someone else collecting the loan.

Is this the person in the document? Compare the selfie with the photo in the ID card or in a digital credential. Useful where documents are reliable; weak where they are not.

Have we seen this person before under another name? Identification, 1:N. Compare a new applicant with every borrower already enrolled. This is the check that stops serial fraud, and the one most onboarding flows skip because it needs a database search rather than a single comparison.

Face or iris?

Face works on any phone, so it is the natural choice for self-service apps and for 1:1 checks. Its weakness is 1:N at scale: as the database grows, look-alikes and family members start producing false hits, and a skilled fraudster changes appearance.

Iris needs a scanner, but it separates people far better, which makes it the right tool for de-duplication. A handheld USB scanner costs a few hundred dollars and plugs into the laptop or Android tablet that an agent or branch already uses. A lender with a network of agents can enrol each borrower once by iris and use face on the phone for everything after that.

Liveness

A face check without liveness detection can be beaten with a printed photo or a video on another phone. At minimum, run a passive liveness model on the selfie; for higher-risk actions add a challenge such as "turn your head left", checked from head pose. Iris capture with a dedicated scanner and an operator present is much harder to fool.

Sharing without sharing data

Fraudsters move between lenders. A borrower blocked by one microfinance institution goes to the next one. The useful answer is a shared check: can lenders ask "has this person got an open loan elsewhere?" without handing each other names, photos or iris images? Iris codes help here, because they are compact, standard and cannot be turned back into a picture. Our INGRESS project, funded by the EU's NGI ONTOCHAIN programme, built this kind of biometric credit bureau for microlending.

Doing it on a small budget

  • Onboarding app: face capture, passive liveness and selfie-to-document comparison on the phone with the open-source Iriscan Biometric SDK (Android, iOS, React Native). No per-check fees, and images do not have to leave the device.
  • Agent or branch: an iris scanner and the Edge service, which keeps working when the connection drops and syncs later.
  • Back office: 1:N search with the Cloud Iris API or the same matcher on your own servers if regulation requires data to stay in the country.

Start by measuring: enrol a few thousand existing borrowers and run de-duplication against them. The number of duplicates you find is the business case.
