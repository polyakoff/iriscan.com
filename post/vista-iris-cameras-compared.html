Vista EY2, EY2P, EY2H and FA2: Dual vs Single Iris Cameras Compared

Vista Imaging makes four iris cameras that we supply: the EY2, the EY2-02 (EY2P), the handheld EY2H and the FA2. They share the same core, near-infrared iris capture with ISO/IEC 19794-6 images and built-in anti-spoofing, and differ in how many eyes they capture at once, whether they take a face photo and where they are meant to stand. This comparison should help you pick one for enrollment or verification.

All four connect over USB 2.0, use multi-wavelength infrared illumination and include LiveEye anti-spoof detection. The manufacturer lists Windows and Linux support.

Dual or single iris?

For enrollment and de-duplication, capture both eyes. Two independent irises make a 1:N search far more robust: requiring both to agree cuts false matches by orders of magnitude, and if one eye is injured or covered the other still identifies the person. A dual camera takes both in one look, which also shortens the queue.

For verification at a later visit, one eye is usually enough. The person claims an identity and the camera confirms it, so a smaller, cheaper single-iris camera such as the FA2 does the job at a counter or a clinic desk.

Face photo or not?

A face image taken in the same capture is useful for the card, for an operator's visual check and as a fallback when iris capture is impossible. The EY2 and EY2P take it at the same time as the irises; the EY2H leaves it out to stay light and handheld.

Which one where

  • Registration desk, national or program enrollment: EY2 or EY2P.
  • Mobile teams and field registration: EY2H.
  • Branch counter, clinic reception, kiosk verification: FA2.
  • Agents with a laptop or Android tablet and a tight budget: a monocular USB iris scanner, see pricing.
