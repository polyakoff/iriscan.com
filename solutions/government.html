Solutions · Government: elections and borders

One person, one vote, one identity at the border

Biometric registration, de-duplication and verification for electoral commissions, civil registries and border agencies, built on open standards and kept under the state's control.

Discuss your program Read: lessons from Nigeria, the UAE and the EU

In plain words

A public register should hold each person once. With paper lists and document checks, the same person can sign up twice at two offices, by mistake or on purpose, and a borrowed document can pass at a counter.

  1. Registration. The officer scans the person's eyes along with the usual photo and fingerprints. The kit works without internet.
  2. One person, one record. The system compares the newcomer with everyone already in the register. A second registration is set aside for an officer to decide on.
  3. At the polling station or the border. The person looks into the scanner and is matched to their record in seconds, even when their fingerprints are hard to read.

An example with round numbers

Take a register that pays a monthly allowance of $20 to 100,000 people. Suppose 1 name in 200 belongs to someone registered twice. That is 500 extra payments, or $10,000 every month. For scale, a register of this size costs $300 a month in our cloud, plus $50 to $190 for each USB scanner at a registration office; on your own servers the licence starts at $0.03 per person a year, $3,000 a year for this register. These figures are only an example; a pilot measures the real share of duplicates in your register.

The problem

What makes a public register trustworthy? Each person should appear in it once, and the person who votes, crosses the border or receives a benefit should be the person in the record. Paper registers and document checks fail at both: people register twice, under-age and fake records stay in the list, and a borrowed or forged document passes at the checkpoint.

What you get

Biometric registration

Mobile enrollment kits for registration centers and field teams capture iris, face and fingerprints, work offline and synchronize later. The same kits serve voter, civil and residence registration.

De-duplication of the register

Every new record is searched against the whole register. Double registrations and fake records are flagged for adjudication before the register is published.

Verification at the checkpoint

At a polling station or a border post the person is matched to the record in seconds. Iris helps many people whose fingerprints fail, one of the reasons for failed accreditations in past elections.

Watch lists and audit

Border systems check arrivals against watch lists and previous entries. Every match and every manual decision is logged, so the process can be reviewed by observers and courts.

Why iris

A national register needs a modality that separates people well enough to search millions of records without flooding adjudicators with false matches, and that works for the whole population, including farmers and the elderly. Iris has been used at the UAE border since 2001 for exactly this kind of exhaustive search. We recommend it together with face and fingerprint, so the register stays compatible with existing systems and with the face and fingerprint data in travel documents.

Sovereignty and standards

Open standards, no vendor lock

Biometric images are stored in ISO formats, and our components integrate with MOSIP, the open-source identity platform used by national ID programs. When the contract ends, the register stays with you. See database migration.

Data stays in the country

Everything runs in your data center, with encryption, access logs and retention rules set by your law. We provide software, integration and training for your own team.
