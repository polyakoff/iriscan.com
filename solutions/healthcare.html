Solutions · Hospitals and medical records

One patient, one record, the right treatment

Biometric patient identification for hospitals, clinic networks and national health record programs, where names repeat, dates of birth are approximate and documents are easy to borrow.

Discuss your hospital Read: duplicate records and impersonation

The problem

How does a registration desk find the right record? Usually by name, date of birth and sometimes a card number. When two patients share a name, the wrong record opens. When the desk finds nothing, a second record is created, and the patient's history is split in two. When a patient uses someone else's card, the history of two people is mixed in one. Each of these errors ends up at the bedside or in a claim.

What you get

Identification at registration

The patient looks at a scanner, and the system returns the record. No spelling of names, no searching by date of birth, no card to forget.

No duplicate records

Every new registration is searched against all enrolled patients, so a returning patient cannot get a second record by accident or on purpose. Existing duplicates surface as patients come back.

Checks where errors are made

The same identification before medication, transfusion, sample collection and procedures, on a tablet at the bedside or a scanner in the lab.

Verified encounters for billing

The visit record carries the biometric check, so claims to insurers and national schemes are backed by proof that the patient was there. See health insurance.

Why iris

A hospital needs contactless identification, and it needs it to work for the elderly, for manual workers with worn fingerprints and for patients who cannot hold a finger still. Iris is captured from a distance, does not change with age and separates people well enough to search a whole regional patient register. Face is available as a second modality for patients who cannot open their eyes for the scanner.

Deployment

Integration

An API and SDK for your hospital information system or EMR, including open-source systems, and an identity layer that can be shared by a network of facilities. USB scanners at desks, tablets in wards, offline mode for rural clinics with the Edge service.

Where the data lives

On hospital or ministry servers. Biometric templates are kept apart from medical records and linked by a pseudonymous identifier, so a leak of one database does not expose the other.

How we start

We recommend starting with the registration desks of one facility. For a few weeks returning patients are enrolled as they come, and the system reports how many of them already have more than one record. This number is usually the clearest argument for the rest of the rollout.
