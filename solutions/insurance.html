Make sure the policy stays with the person who bought it

Biometric verification for life, funeral and microinsurance at sale, at policy changes, at claim and at payout, through agents, branches and mobile apps.

In plain words

An agent sells a funeral policy. Years later someone comes to collect the payout, and nobody at your office has ever seen this person. The ID on the form looks right, but it cannot tell you whether this is the beneficiary the policyholder chose.

  1. At sale. The agent scans the eyes of the policyholder and of the beneficiaries they name. It takes a few seconds per person.
  2. At any change. A new bank account or a new beneficiary is accepted only after the policyholder looks into the scanner again.
  3. At claim. The person collecting the money is scanned too. You pay when the system confirms it is the beneficiary on the policy.

An example with round numbers

Suppose a branch sells 3,000 funeral policies a year with an average payout of $1,500. Assume that 5 claims a year are paid to the wrong person or on a policy sold to someone who does not exist. That is $7,500 a year. The first year of checks costs $1,378 without VAT: one $190 scanner and the cloud at $99 a month. These are example numbers. Keep in mind that the saving on claims comes years after the sale, while changes of bank account are protected from the first day.

The problem

Who is on the other side of a policy? An insurer meets the policyholder at sale, often through an agent, and then again years later, when someone comes with a claim. Sometimes that someone is not the insured at all, but a brother or a cousin with the same policy. In between, beneficiaries and bank accounts change, and every change is a chance to take over the policy. An ID number on a form proves little at any of these moments if the document can be borrowed or forged.

What you get

Verification at sale

The policyholder is enrolled with iris or face when the policy is sold, and the system checks the new person against everyone already enrolled. A policy for a person who does not exist, or for the same person under a second name, is flagged at enrollment.

Protected policy changes

A change of beneficiary, bank account or contact details requires the policyholder to confirm with the same biometric. This closes an easy route to taking over someone else's policy.

Claimant verification

Beneficiaries are enrolled when they are named, so at claim time the insurer can check that the person collecting the payout is the person the policyholder chose.

Proof of life for annuities

Annuitants and pensioners confirm they are alive with a short scan at a branch, an agent or on the phone, instead of a paper life certificate that anyone can bring.

Why iris

A national register answers the question "does this ID number exist?", and where it supports biometrics, "does this face match the photo on record?". It does not tell the insurer that the person in front of the agent is the same person who signed the policy ten years ago. Iris stays usable for decades, does not wear like fingerprints of manual workers and separates people well enough to de-duplicate a whole portfolio. We use it together with face, so the remote flows on the phone and the assisted flows at agents use the same identity.

Deployment

Channels

Face capture with liveness inside your app with the open-source Biometric SDK, USB iris scanners for branches and agents, rugged BIOSID tablets for field sales. Offline agents work through the Edge service.

Where the data lives

On your servers or with our Cloud Iris API, with biometric images in ISO formats. Biometric data is stored apart from policy records, encrypted and pseudonymized, with a defined retention period.
