Solutions · Biometric database migration

Change the vendor, keep every identity

Audit and conversion of biometric databases between vendors and standards, for national ID, voter, health and banking registers that should not be held hostage by one supplier.

Discuss your migration Read: migration without vendor lock

In plain words

Your register has run on one supplier's system for years, and now it has to move to another. The question that matters is how many people the new system will fail to recognize. Today nobody can give you that number.

  1. Look before you move. We take a sample of a few thousand records from different years and offices and check what is really inside them.
  2. Convert. Records are rewritten in open standard formats that any supplier can read. Where the old format cannot be moved, the record is rebuilt from the stored fingerprint or iris image.
  3. Test before the switch. The same people are searched in the old system and in the new one. Anyone the new system misses is found now, not later at a service counter.

An example with round numbers

Suppose your register holds 1,000,000 people and 2% of the records will not work in the new system. That is 20,000 people. Without an audit you meet them one at a time, when they are turned away after the switch. If calling one person back to re-enroll costs your office $5 in staff time, those 20,000 cost $100,000. Records that still have a usable image can be rebuilt instead, so only the rest need a visit. All of this is an example; the audit gives you the real share for your register.

What you need to start

A sample of records and a secure place to work on them: inside your data center, or as an encrypted batch sent to our cloud. The audit takes a few weeks, and we quote it once we know the size and formats of your register. You can try the converter in your browser before that; nothing is uploaded.

The problem

What do you actually own after ten years with one biometric vendor? Often a database whose templates only that vendor's matcher can read, images in a variant of a standard that other systems reject, and personal data hidden inside the files. When the contract ends, the hardware is outdated or the regulator changes the format, all of this has to move, and nobody can be lost on the way.

What you get

Database audit

We inventory what is really stored: formats and their vendor variants, image quality, templates that cannot be moved, duplicates and personal data inside biometric files. You get a report before any decision about the new vendor.

Batch conversion

Fingerprint and iris records converted between vendor variants of ISO/IEC 19794, ANSI/NIST-ITL, ANSI 378 and ISO card formats, with templates re-extracted from images where proprietary templates cannot be reused.

Verification before switch-over

A sample is matched on the old and the new system, and differences are explained before the new system goes live. Missing identities are found before cutover, not after.

Privacy clean-up

Biographic data embedded in biometric files is extracted or removed, so the converted database stores only what it should. This supports data portability and minimization under GDPR and similar laws.

Why us

Our BDI converter is built on NIST's open-source NBIS and BiomDI libraries, and its fingerprint conversion engine is open source on GitHub. You can try it on the biometric data conversion page: the same engine runs in your browser and converts a fingerprint image to an ISO or ANSI template without uploading anything. We are not a matcher vendor in your tender, so we have no interest in locking the converted data to anyone.

Deployment

On your premises

For national registers the conversion runs inside your data center or in an isolated environment, and no biometric data leaves it. We provide the software, scripts and engineers.

As a service

For smaller databases, encrypted batches are converted in our cloud and returned with a conversion report, including the records that could not be converted and why.

How we start

We recommend starting with an audit of a representative sample, usually a few thousand records from different years and enrollment sites. In a few weeks you know which share of the database converts cleanly, which records need re-extraction from images and which need re-enrollment. This gives you a realistic plan and a stronger position in negotiations with the new vendor.

Changing a biometric vendor or format?

Tell us the size of the database, the modalities and the current vendor. We will propose an audit and a migration plan.
