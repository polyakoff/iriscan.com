Know who you lend to, and let borrowers know it too

Biometric onboarding and de-duplication for lenders, fintechs and agent networks. A borrower who has been scanned once is recognized under any name, and knowing that changes how people repay.

Discuss your use case Read: guide for lenders

In plain words

A client who never repaid comes back a year later. He has a new phone number, a different ID card, perhaps a slightly different spelling of his name. Your loan officer has no way to tell, and the money goes out again.

  1. First visit. The loan officer holds a small scanner in front of the client's eyes for a few seconds.
  2. Check. The system compares those eyes with every client you have scanned before. This is de-duplication: finding the same person twice.
  3. Answer. A new client gets the loan as usual. A known one shows up with their old record and repayments, whatever name is on today's form.

An example with round numbers

Suppose you issue 2,000 loans a year, $300 each on average, and 2 in every 100 go to someone who already defaulted with you under another name. That is 40 loans, or $12,000 a year you will not see again. The first year of iris checks costs $1,881 without VAT: the $990 starter kit with the first three months, then $99 a month. Stop only half of those loans and you keep $6,000, about three times the cost. The numbers are made up for illustration; try it with your own.

The problem

How does a lender know that a new applicant is not a former borrower under another name? A document shows a name and a photo, but documents can be borrowed or bought, and many first-time borrowers have none. The loss is specific: the same person takes loans under different names and repays none of them.

The effect starts before the first match

Why did repayment grow in Malawi? The lender did not share data with anyone. Borrowers simply knew that this lender would recognize them under any name, so a default could not be left behind by coming back as someone else. The researchers found that the riskiest borrowers took smaller loans and diverted less of the inputs the loans were meant for. Ghana's national health insurer described the same mechanism: an official called its biometric system psychologically a deterrent. A scan at application is a clear signal to the borrower, and this effect does not depend on the size of the database.

What you get

Onboarding on the phone

Face capture, passive liveness and selfie-to-document comparison inside your app with the open-source Biometric SDK (Android, iOS, React Native). No per-check fees, and images do not have to leave the device.

De-duplication in your portfolio

Each new applicant is searched against every borrower you have enrolled. A second account under another name is flagged before the loan is issued, and existing duplicates surface as borrowers come back.

Agents and branches offline

A USB iris scanner on the laptop or Android tablet the agent already uses, with the Edge service. It keeps working without a connection and synchronizes later.

A history tied to the person

Repayment history in your portfolio belongs to the person, not to a document number. A good borrower keeps their record when they change a phone or an ID card, and a defaulter cannot start again with a clean name.

Duplicate borrower detection for microfinance and loan agents

Most duplicate borrowers are not caught at head office but missed in the field: an agent opens an account for a person who already has one at another branch, under a slightly different name or with a borrowed document. With an iris scanner on the agent's laptop or tablet, every application is checked against the whole portfolio before the loan is approved. A match shows the existing borrower and their repayment record; no match means a genuinely new client. How it works for the borrower

  1. Enroll once. At the first visit the agent scans the borrower's eye. The system checks that this person is not already registered with you and saves the iris template to the profile.
  2. Confirm identity in seconds. Next time the borrower looks at the camera, and the system returns the profile. For routine operations no ID card is needed.
  3. Get the loan. The decision is based on a verified identity and a repayment history that cannot be moved to a new name.

Our Cloud Iris API for a quick start, or the same matcher on your servers if regulation requires data to stay in the country. Iris and face images are stored in ISO formats, so any other vendor can build its own templates from them, and you are not tied to us.

How we start

We recommend a short pilot: enroll a few thousand existing borrowers at several agents and run de-duplication against them. In a few weeks you get the number of duplicate identities in your portfolio, the false rejection rate on your own population and hardware, and the cost per check. New applicants are enrolled at the same time, so you can compare their repayment with borrowers who joined before. This gives you the business case before any large investment.

Next: a shared check between lenders

Fraudsters move between lenders, and one lender's database cannot see that. When several lenders use the same identification, they can ask "does this person have an open loan elsewhere?" without exchanging names, photos or iris images. In the EU-funded INGRESS project we built a prototype of it: a biometric bureau that issues verifiable credentials bound to a person, and a credit bureau that keeps loan records. It makes sense once several lenders in a market take part, and the template enrolled in your pilot is the same one it will use.
